## Hook

We mapped every manual step our client's ops team did in a week. Here's what we found.

---

## Beat 1 — BEFORE: the hidden ops tax

Eight people. One week. We asked each person on the ops team to log every manual action they took that wasn't actual delivery work — status emails, follow-up pings, copy-pasting data between tools, reformatting spreadsheets, answering "where is this?" Slack messages.

What came back surprised even them.

In a single week:
- 47 status update emails sent manually (same info, different recipients)
- 23 "just checking in" follow-ups on overdue tasks — all manually written
- ~3 hours of copy-paste between their CRM, Airtable, and invoicing tool
- 6 recurring reports built by hand from data that already existed in a database

Across the team: **14 hours of ops time spent per week on information movement.** Not decisions. Not delivery. Moving information from one place to another because no one had built a system to do it automatically.

---

## Beat 2 — WHAT WE BUILT

Four targeted automations, each addressing a specific finding from the audit:

1. **Automated status reporting** — Make pulls task status from Airtable every Monday morning and sends a formatted digest to each stakeholder's Slack channel. No one writes a status email anymore.

2. **Overdue task follow-up sequence** — When a task hits its due date with no completion logged, a follow-up message fires automatically to the assignee at 24h and 72h. The PM no longer tracks what's overdue manually.

3. **Cross-tool data sync** — A bidirectional sync between their CRM and invoicing tool eliminated the 3 hours/week of manual data entry entirely. Deal closes in the CRM; the invoice populates automatically.

4. **Report generation pipeline** — Weekly reports now pull from the same Airtable database and generate formatted Google Docs automatically. The only human action: review and send.

Total build time: 3 weeks. Zero new hires.

---

## Beat 3 — RESULT

14 hours/week recovered across an 8-person team. In a month, that's 56 hours — the equivalent of a full week of ops capacity returned to the business without touching headcount.

The follow-up rate on overdue tasks improved because the system is consistent in a way that humans aren't. Status emails got cleaner because they're now generated from live data rather than assembled from memory.

The ops lead's comment at the 30-day check-in: "I stopped being a traffic cop."

---

## Close

Most ops inefficiency isn't a people problem — it's a system problem. The team was spending 14 hours a week moving information that could have been moving itself.

The first step is always the audit. Map the manual steps. The automations become obvious.
