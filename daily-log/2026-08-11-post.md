## Beat 1 — BEFORE: the invisible PM tax

Most agency PMs spend somewhere between 30–50% of their week on coordination overhead — not delivery work. Pulling status from Asana, translating it into a client-facing email, updating the Slack channel, answering "where are we on X" for the third time this week.

This is invisible cost because it looks like "managing the project." It isn't. It's manually moving information from one place to another.

We scoped a project management audit for an agency with 4 PMs and 12 active client accounts. The result: each PM was spending ~12 hours per week on status reporting alone. 4 PMs × 12 hours = 48 hours/week of senior-level time spent copying numbers into emails.

---

## Beat 2 — WHAT WE BUILT

A PM automation layer that runs on top of their existing project management tool (in this case, Asana + Slack + HubSpot):

1. **Milestone trigger**: when a task is marked complete in Asana, an automation checks if it's a milestone-level task
2. **Auto-status update**: if it is, a pre-formatted Slack message posts to the internal #project-updates channel with client name, milestone, and next due date — no PM input needed
3. **Client email**: for client-facing milestones, an email drafts automatically in HubSpot and pings the PM for a one-click approve-and-send (PM reviews, doesn't write)
4. **Weekly status report**: every Friday at 4pm, an automated summary compiles open tasks, completed milestones, and next week's priorities per client — sent to each client account directly from HubSpot

Total PM time per client per week on status reporting: from ~3 hours → 20 minutes (review + one-click approve on pre-drafted emails).

---

## Beat 3 — RESULT

4 PMs. 12 active accounts. Before: 48 hours/week of status-coordination overhead. After: 16 hours/week — 32 hours returned to actual delivery work.

Within 6 weeks, the agency onboarded 3 new clients without hiring a fifth PM. The project management capacity was already there — it was just buried in copy-paste work.

---

## Close

The bottleneck isn't your project manager's ability. It's the system that routes every piece of status information through them manually.

Automate the routing. Let the PM manage the exceptions.
